# The phone rules

Everything in this file is a rule the root `CLAUDE.md` states in one line and
sends you here for. **Read it before doing responsive work** — changing a
breakpoint, a `touch:` affordance, a phone layout, the dock's auto-hide, or
anything anchored to the bottom of the window. Each entry carries the reason
it exists and, usually, the thing that was tried first and didn't work; that
is the half that stops a fix being re-broken six weeks later.

Above `md` almost none of it applies, and that is deliberate — the desktop
layout should be untouched by construction, not by a JS media query.

- **A batch strip is ONE line that scrolls, at every width** (B-Roll's two storyboard strips). Four pills wrapped to two or three rows at 375px, which is 40px of chrome per extra row on the screen with the least to give; the work reads as a sequence anyway, so swiping along it is closer to what it is than stacking it. The row takes `w-full overflow-x-auto scrollbar-hide` with `-mx-5 px-5` (so it scrolls edge to edge while its first and last pills sit on the panel's own inset) and each pill takes `shrink-0`. `w-max min-w-full` on the inner row is what lets it be *placed* and scrollable at once: at `min-w-full` the row is exactly the port when it fits, so a `flex-1` spacer (or `justify-center`) can do its work, and exactly its content when it doesn't, so the spacer collapses and the scroll starts at pill one. A plain `justify-center` inside a scroll port centres the OVERFLOW too, which strands the first pill off the left edge with no way to scroll back to it. **The Line-by-Line strip is the current shape**: style pill left, actions right, meta and buttons on the same line at every width — which works only because NOTHING on the line shrinks (the meta included), so the collision that drove the meta off this bar twice before simply can't be expressed. Its three generate passes live behind one **Generate all** menu; three long labels were ~450px, most of the panel at every width the right pane actually gets, and they're mutually exclusive in practice. Continuous' strip has not been brought across yet.

- `components/MobilePaneTabs.tsx` — **on a phone a two-pane app shows ONE pane at a time.** Every workspace app is a desktop split: what you fill in on the left, what comes out on the right. Stacking those two columns under `md` was tried first and doesn't work — each panel is built as a full-height column with its own internal scroll and a pinned footer, so stacked they collapse into a pair of ~200px scroll windows with the Generate bar lying across the fields it belongs to, and the output pane sits below a fold nobody finds. The bar is `md:hidden` and the panes swap with `hidden md:flex` (`paneClass`, same file), so there is no JS media query and the desktop layout is untouched by construction — `paneClass` deliberately sets nothing on `flex` above `md`, leaving each app's own `md:w-1/2` / `md:flex-1` to win. Wired in Characters (Controls / Gallery), Scripts (Setup / Output), Voiceovers (Script / Voice), B-Roll (Setup / Storyboard), Playground (Prompt / History) and Ad Analyzer (Analyses / Analysis). **Firing Generate flips to the output pane** — on a phone that's the only way to see the thing you just paid for.

- `hooks/useChromeAutoHide.ts` + `stores/chromeStore.ts` — **on a phone, scrolling down rolls the DOCK away and a sustained scroll up hands it back. Nothing else moves.** A phone gives the workspace ~812px and the dock takes ~98 of them before an app draws a pixel. `useChromeAutoHide` (mounted once in `App.tsx`) listens for scroll in the **capture phase on window** — scroll doesn't bubble but it does capture, so one listener covers every scroller in the app without each one opting in — and only answers to scrollers inside `[data-app-pane="active"]`, so a body-portaled modal's own scrolling can't strand the dock hidden behind it. `NEVER_HIDE_IN` exempts whole apps — Edit and the Dashboard are one screen each, where hiding the dock hands the pane ~98px it has no use for and the page visibly jumps under a thumb that was only nudging it.
  **A panel's own toggle header lives OUTSIDE its phone scroller.** Four input panels made their ROOT the `max-md:overflow-y-auto` box (so the fields and the Generate band read as one page), which put the `h-[57px]` tab bar inside it — and that bar then scrolled away with the fields, taking the way back to the other tab with it. B-Roll, Characters, Scripts and Playground all now keep the header as a sibling and wrap everything below it in `<div className="flex min-h-0 flex-1 flex-col max-md:overflow-y-auto">`. Above `md` that wrapper is inert and each panel's own inner scroller still does the work.
  **The dock is the only thing that hides, and that boundary is the whole design** (Massimo's call, August 2026, after two passes took too much). The menu bar stays: `App.tsx`'s pane insets go `bottom-[~98px]` → `bottom-0` and the top stays at `top-9` in every state, because the menu bar is `fixed` and opaque, so a pane that also claimed its 36px only slid each app's **first row** out of sight behind it — the Setup/Storyboard tabs on a two-pane app, the platform tabs in Outliers. And nothing inside an app hides either: a `CollapsingBar` wrapped the search / filter / sort rows for a week (Outliers' vault and platform filters, B-Roll's history search + mode pills + sort, Scripts' and Voiceovers' history search, the Ad Analyzer rail's phone row) and the component is **deleted** — a bar that rolls away going down and unrolls coming back up moves the list under the thumb reading it, and every one of those rows is now a plain static row. `MobilePaneTabs` and the `h-[57px]` panel headers had already lost theirs for the older version of the same reason: that bar is how you reach the other half of the app. **Don't reintroduce any of it** — the phone's answer to a crowded surface is a shorter bar (see the vault's two-row filter panel), not a bar that comes and goes.
  **A scroll the app started itself is not a gesture either** (`suspendChromeAutoHide`, same file). An in-app jump — the Ad Analyzer's Breakdown / Transcript / Scenes toggle, Characters' tab jump, `ProductForm`'s section jump, Scripts' scroll to Generate — travels most of a screen in one direction, which from inside the tracker is indistinguishable from a deliberate swipe down. So the dock hid MID-ANIMATION, the pane's inset went 98px → 0, and the browser's own smooth scroll relayouted underneath itself: measured on the Ad Analyzer at 375px, a jump to Scenes landed **670px past** its target from the top of the read and did **nothing at all** from halfway down. Every such call site announces itself and the dock simply holds still for the length of the jump — which is right anyway, since the member tapped a tab rather than swiping. **Any new programmatic scroll inside the pane has to call it.**
  Four things keep the dock from twitching. Direction has to be sustained, and **asymmetrically** — `HIDE_TRAVEL_PX` is a nudge (40px) where `SHOW_TRAVEL_PX` is about a third of a screen (260px), because showing the dock puts it back over the Generate button the member was reaching for, so a false positive costs more than a missed one; the accumulator resets on every direction change. The top of a scroller always shows it. It won't hide for a scroller with less than `MIN_OVERFLOW_PX` of overflow. And **a scroll nobody made is not a gesture**: every flip mutes the tracker for `SETTLE_MS` (hiding the dock makes the pane taller, which shortens every scroller inside it, so a list parked near its end is CLAMPED back up by the browser — without the mute that reads as "scrolled up", which shows the dock, which shrinks the pane again, and the dock flaps once a frame), and `lastMax` catches the same clamp arriving late or from elsewhere (a mobile URL bar, the keyboard closing, a lazily-sized tile resolving) by discarding an upward delta no bigger than the height the scroller just LOST. That test is deliberately narrow rather than "any event where the extent changed": the grids run `content-visibility: auto`, so their `scrollHeight` moves under every scroll and the blunter version threw real gestures away. The inset flip is **deliberately not transitioned** — `bottom` is layout, and relayouting a whole storyboard every frame for 300ms is the one thing this was supposed to buy back. Above `md` none of it applies.

- **`touch:` is how a hover-only affordance stays reachable** (`@custom-variant touch (@media (hover: none))`, `index.css`). On a phone there is no pointer to hover with and a tap goes to whatever the tile itself does, so a `group-hover` reveal isn't merely hidden there — it's unreachable. Two shapes, and which one to use depends on how many controls are behind it. A LONE control just appears: `touch:opacity-100` on the remove button of a reference thumbnail or a frame slot, the Bank's "Animate in Playground" pill, the Voiceovers history row actions and its scrubber thumb, the character name on a gallery tile. A COLUMN of them gets a **doorway** instead — `TileActionStack` renders a touch-only `⋯` that opens the stack (`TouchDisclosure`, `components/tileActions.tsx`), because showing all four circles is 140px of chrome down the side of a tile, permanently, over the still it exists to let you judge. The disclosure carries its own `display` rather than a `useIsTouch()`: under a pointer it is `display: none`, so it can never be pressed, `open` can never become true, and the hover behaviour is untouched by construction. **A `touch:` that MOVES something has to move whatever was clearing it too.** B-Roll's centred type chip dropped to `touch:top-11` to get out of the top corners (where the touch disclosure is permanent and a video cover's play button sits opposite it) — and the text face under it kept the `pt-9` that cleared the chip's desktop position, so on a phone the tag sat squarely across the second line of the prompt. **Both came back to the top row in August 2026**: the phone storyboard is `grid-cols-2`, which leaves a clear lane between the two corner buttons, and a chip a row down read as floating in the middle of the still rather than labelling it. The two numbers are still a pair; change one and check the other. While the stack is shut its children take `pointer-events-none` on touch — an `opacity-0` button still swallows taps, which under a pointer never shows because hovering reveals the column anyway.

- **`max-md:` is where a growing box stops sharing height.** The input columns hand their leftover height to the boxes that grow (`flex-1`, `flex-1 basis-0`, `flex-[5]`). On a phone there IS no leftover — the column is shorter than its own contents — so `flex-1` just squeezes every box until the card's `overflow-hidden` slices it. Under `md` those boxes take `max-md:flex-none` plus a real `max-md:min-h-[…]` floor and the column scrolls. The floor still lives on the section, exactly as on desktop. The same shortage decides where a **generate band** goes: on a desktop it's the column's pinned footer, but on a phone a column that's already too short for its own fields can't also lend the bottom of the screen to a settings row and a button — so B-Roll's band moved INSIDE the scroll (August 2026, a `max-md:overflow-y-auto` wrapper around the inputs column and the band; the inputs column keeps its own `md:overflow-y-auto`). It is simply the end of the page there: fill the fields in, keep scrolling, press Generate. The `h-[57px]` header above it stays outside the wrapper and stays pinned.

- **A wide data table becomes a CARD LIST below `md`, never a sideways scroller** (Admin's Members and Allowlist, August 2026). A ten-column table has nowhere to go on a phone: made to scroll horizontally, the name is off screen the moment you reach for the status, which is the one pairing the table exists to show. So the `<table>` takes `hidden md:block` and a `md:hidden` list of cards renders beside it — identity and status on the first line, the remaining columns as a two-up meta grid, then the row's own actions at full card width (a 24px text button is a desktop target). Two things the table gave away for free have to be rebuilt: **sort**, which lived in the clickable column headers and comes back as a `Dropdown` + direction toggle on its own phone row, and **select-all**, which lived in the header checkbox and joins that row. Long values that identify a record (an email) wrap with `break-all` rather than truncate — the local part is the half you're reading. Keep the actions in ONE shared component used by both shapes, or the table and the card drift apart.

- **A bare `grid` is a horizontal-overflow bug waiting to happen.** With no `grid-template-columns` the children land in an *implicit* `auto` track whose floor is the widest child's **min-content** — and a `truncate` span's min-content is its whole nowrap sentence, since `min-w-0` is a floor override, not a cap on intrinsic contribution. Admin's announcement editor (`grid gap-5 lg:grid-cols-[…]`, unstyled below `lg`) grew its one track past the viewport and scrolled the entire pane sideways, taking the panel's left inset with it. Always declare the small-screen columns too — `grid-cols-1` is `repeat(1, minmax(0,1fr))`, which caps the track. And prefer **wrapping a hint under its label** on a phone over truncating it: a hint you can't read is chrome.

- **Fields render at 13px under `md`, and the viewport is capped at `maximum-scale=1`** (one rule at the bottom of `index.css`, one meta tag in `index.html` — they are ONE fix and only work together). iOS Safari zooms the page in whenever a focused field's font-size is under 16px and never zooms back out, so the app's 12–14px chrome type made every prompt box a one-way trip into a zoomed viewport with the dock off screen. That was bought off with a blanket 16px until August 2026, and the biggest boxes paid for it: B-Roll's 13px script field rendering at 16px is a fifth fewer lines of a pasted script, on the screen where lines matter most. The viewport cap holds the zoom off instead — it does not take pinch-zoom away, since Safari has ignored scale caps for user-initiated gestures since iOS 10 — which frees the fields to render at the app's own field size. It is a FLOOR, not a size: nothing on a phone is smaller than a desktop field and nothing is bigger, and the rule only lifts the 11–12px compact controls to something a thumb can hit. Don't put 16px back without also removing the cap. **The old blanket rule was still in the file for a month after the floor landed and silently won the whole time** — `font-size: 16px !important` on `input, textarea, select` under 1024px, from the first commit — because `!important` outranks the floor whatever the source order, so nothing about #501 actually reached a phone until it was deleted (August 2026, reported as "the text is a bit too big in all the forms"). This rule is also **unlayered**, where every Tailwind utility sits in `@layer utilities`, so it beats a `text-base` on the element too: the field size on a phone is set HERE and nowhere else. **A highlight mirror is a DIV and the rule can't reach it, so it opts in with `.field-mirror`** — declared in the same block, because a mirror that renders a point off its textarea wraps on a different character and every wash slides further off its word the further down the paragraph you read. `TokenField` carried its own `max-lg:text-[16px]` — the size the floor used to impose, at a breakpoint it no longer uses — and painted Scripts' `[CHARACTER]` washes from a 16px copy of a 13px paragraph, landing them lines away from their tokens (fixed August 2026); `BracketHighlightArea` had the milder 14-against-13 version in `ExpandTextModal`. Don't set a phone size on a mirror in its own component.

- **A touch screen reserves no scrollbar gutter** (`@media (hover: none)` at the top of `index.css`, plus an early return in `utils/autoHideScrollbars.ts`). Native webkit scrollbars are kept invisible but 11px wide, so the layout doesn't shift when one appears — and that 11px is real layout, taken out of every scroller's width. On a phone it bought nothing (there's no pointer to drag one with) and cost two things: 11px off every grid, and a visible STEP down the right edge wherever a bar pinned INSIDE a scroller sat under one outside it — the Ad Analyzer's sticky section toggle painted 11px short of the pane while the pane tabs above it ran full width.

- **Nothing rubber-bands** (`overscroll-behavior: none` on `html, body, #root` and on the overflow utilities, `index.css`). Pulling past the end of a scroller on a phone dragged the surface up and showed a band of empty page under it — reported on the Dashboard and Edit, where the pane's own scroller is the one being pulled. It also stops a nested scroller chaining its leftover scroll into its parent, which is the other half of the same feel.

- **Anything anchored to the bottom of the window holds back `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`.** In a browser tab that inset is 0 — the browser's own bar owns the strip — so nothing changes and the numbers are what they always were. INSTALLED to an iPhone home screen it isn't: `viewport-fit=cover` plus no browser bar means the page runs to the physical bottom edge and the inset reports the ~34px the home indicator sits in. The **dock** has always padded itself up by `max(env(safe-area-inset-bottom),0.5rem)`, but `App.tsx`'s workspace pane reserved a flat pixel count for it — nav height plus that floor — so installed, the dock stood ~22px taller than the pane held back and **covered the bottom of every pinned Generate button**. The pane now uses the dock's own expression (`calc(90px+max(env(safe-area-inset-bottom),0.5rem))`), so the two derive from the same numbers and can't drift — change the dock's own padding and this has to move with it; it keeps the bare inset even with the dock rolled away, since content under the home indicator is content you can't reliably touch. `SlideOver` takes `pb-[env(safe-area-inset-bottom)]` on the PANEL (not its footer), so the scrolling list and the footer both end above it. A new bottom-anchored surface needs the same.

- **The app installs as ONE app** (`public/manifest.webmanifest` + the iOS meta tags in `index.html`). Without a manifest, iOS gives a home-screen web clip a scope of the exact URL it was saved at, so the first in-app route change reads as leaving the app and Safari pops its in-app browser chrome over the top — the URL, a back chevron and a reload, on every dock switch. `scope: "/"` covers every route the router can push and `display: standalone` drops the chrome. The status bar is `black`, not `black-translucent`: the menu bar is `fixed top-0` at 36px, and translucent would put the clock and the battery on top of it.

- **`SegmentedToggle`'s `fitContent` takes `'md'`** for a control in a header row that WRAPS on a phone: fit-to-content from `md` up, full-width below. Fitting its content is what keeps a toggle from eating a desktop line it has to share with a search field and a button — but once the header wraps and the toggle owns its own row, shrinking only leaves dead space beside its last label (Outliers' Outlier Vault / TikTok / Meta Ads). It's the container's width AND the segments' `flex-1` in one prop, since the two have to flip together; the indicator follows on its own, because it's already re-measured by a `ResizeObserver`.

- **A full-size `SegmentedToggle` segment runs tighter below `sm`** — `gap-1.5 px-2.5 text-[12.5px]` against the desktop's `gap-2 px-4 text-[13px]`. These are the second-row toggles (three-up section jumps, mode switches), and at 375px they were truncating their own labels for want of exactly that: the Ad Analyzer's read jump rendered as "Break… / Transc… / Scenes". `dense` is already sized for a tight row and is left alone. When a label still won't fit, SHORTEN IT for the phone rather than shrinking the control again — B-Roll's storyboard tab says "Storyboard" there instead of "Line by Line Storyboard", since the mode it qualifies is already picked one row up. `SegmentedToggleOption.label` is a `ReactNode`, so that's two spans and no JS media query.
