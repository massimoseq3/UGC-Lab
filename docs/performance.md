# Compositing and render cost

The rules behind a whole class of "the app feels laggy" reports. The root
`CLAUDE.md` states each in one line and sends you here. **Read it before
adding a `backdrop-filter`, making a bar `sticky`, animating opacity over
generated media, or putting a list beside an input.**

The through-line: this app puts dozens of animating, blurred, generating media
tiles on one screen. A cost that is invisible on one card is the frame budget
on twelve.

- **A history panel next to an input panel is `memo`'d, and its props are stable.** The history banks are uncapped (only `brollHistory` has a cap), so a working member's gallery is hundreds of rows, each with its own asset lookup — and it sits in the same component as the box they're typing into. One fresh callback identity per render means every character typed re-renders the whole list: measured at ~70 ms (Playground) and ~85 ms (Characters) per keystroke at 300 items, versus ~4–10 ms once the subtree is skipped. `PlaygroundHistoryGrid` and Characters' `GalleryPanel` are the two wired this way — `memo` on the panel, `useCallback` with only stable deps on every handler it takes, and the live draft read through a ref rather than closed over. The React Compiler does this on its own for components it can compile, so the explicit `memo` is a belt-and-braces for the app roots that still bail (see Tech Stack).

- **`backdrop-filter` is for small chrome, and `filter: blur()` never animates.** The one rule behind a whole class of "the app feels laggy" reports, fixed in August 2026. A `backdrop-filter` element is a **backdrop root**: a repaint anywhere inside it invalidates its entire backdrop. `App.tsx`'s workspace pane — which contains the whole workspace — carried `backdrop-blur-xl`, so every animating pixel in every app dragged a full-viewport filter recompute behind it. What it bought was nothing: behind it is `AppBackground`, a smooth dithered gradient, and a blurred smooth gradient is the same gradient. It's a plain unpainted pane now (the rounded, bordered floating "window" frame around it came off in August 2026 too — the pane fills the space between the menu bar and the dock flush, so nothing draws a box around the apps), and removing it is why generation animations stopped stuttering, why zoom stopped lagging (a zoom re-rasters everything, backdrops included), and why images stopped looking blocky — a browser under that much compositing pressure drops its raster scale. Keep `backdrop-filter` on small static chrome over real content, and never put one on a full-window container. **`GeneratingBackdrop` is where this rule was finally paid off in full, and how it was done is the point.** Its three drifting blobs each carried a live `blur-2xl`, on a surface whose whole job is to appear a dozen at a time — so a storyboard batch meant three dozen full-size Gaussians per frame. Two earlier goes only bent the curve: rewriting the blobs as radial gradients was reverted within the hour (a gradient's falloff is a linear ramp where a Gaussian's is a smooth curve, so the plateau edge reads as banded lobes instead of one soft wash, and this is a glow whose entire job is to look good while someone waits), and taking the `scale()` out of the `blob-drift-*` keyframes helped without fixing it — the translate alone was still enough to keep every filtered layer dirty. Measured on a grid of backdrops and nothing else, 12 tiles / 24 tiles: **live blur 63 / 36 fps** (worst frame 18ms / 34ms), **one shared filter over all three blobs 114 / 64 fps**, **the Gaussian baked into a mask 120 / 120 fps** — flat, because no filter runs per frame at all. That third one shipped (August 2026). `.gen-blob` masks a flat accent fill with an SVG whose white circle is blurred by a real `feGaussianBlur`: the browser rasterises the mask once and the blob is a picture being moved from then on. It is the same Gaussian curve as before, drawn ahead of time — **not** the gradient that was reverted, so don't "simplify" it into one. Two things it costs: a mask cannot paint outside its element the way a filter can, so the blob boxes are ~2.1× the old ones around the same centres and the mask's circle and those boxes have to be resized together; and if the blur ever needs to change, change the mask's `stdDeviation`, never put `filter` back on something that moves. `offscreen-idle` (`content-visibility: auto`, #459) still skips tiles that are off screen, which now also defers the mask raster until one scrolls in. Its base gradient carries a **faint** accent tint over the ink ramp rather than neutral grey (Massimo's call) — a full accent wash was tried and was too much; the grey is what makes the tile read as frosted glass rather than as a coloured card.

  The other half landed with it: **no `backdrop-filter` may sit on top of this backdrop.** The mode glyph and the cancel/dismiss buttons on every generating tile wore `backdrop-blur-sm`, and a backdrop-filter re-samples and re-blurs its patch on every frame the thing behind it moves — one per generating tile, all of them over an animating surface. They take a slightly heavier flat scrim instead, which reads the same over a soft wash.

- **Chrome that must not move lives OUTSIDE the scroller, not `sticky` inside it.** B-Roll's two storyboard strips were `sticky top-0` glass bars inside the scroll port, and they visibly came loose from the top edge on the way back up a long storyboard: a backdrop-filter element re-samples its backdrop on the main thread while the scroller is scrolled by the compositor, so the bar lagged its own container by a frame or two and snapped back. Both are static `shrink-0` rows above the scroll port now — a sibling of the scrolling box, exactly like the panel header above them — which makes the pinning structural rather than something the compositor has to keep agreeing to. Ask first whether the bar ever actually scrolls away: neither of these did (both were pinned at `top-0` from the first pixel), so `sticky` was buying nothing but the artifact. The cost is that content no longer passes under them blurred, so they take the **opaque** fill (glass over a panel background that never moves is a backdrop root's cost for no picture) and keep `relative z-20` so a card's own positioned hover chrome can't paint over them. When a bar genuinely does have to scroll away and come back, `sticky` is still the tool — just don't also make it glass.

- **Pinned panel chrome is glass, and anything that FADES on hover is not.** Two halves of one backdrop-filter rule. A bar that stays put over content it doesn't scroll with (B-Roll's panel header) takes `supports-[backdrop-filter]:bg-surface-0/80 + backdrop-blur-xl + backdrop-saturate-150` over an opaque `bg-surface-0` fallback: content passing under it blurred is what makes it read as a fixed pane of the panel rather than a card-shaped hole, and a nested backdrop-filter DOES blur inside a parent `backdrop-blur-xl` (the earlier "it can't re-blur" note was wrong — what made a translucent strip read as scrolling was too little blur and no saturation). The opposite holds for anything animating its own opacity: a backdrop-filter re-samples its backdrop every frame, so a hover-faded overlay over a real generated still stutters — which is why `TileActionStack` has never had one, and why B-Roll's variation-card shortcut row, tag chip and status badges dropped theirs (an empty card never showed it; a flat face is nothing to blur).
