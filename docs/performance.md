# Compositing and render cost

The rules behind a whole class of "the app feels laggy" reports. The root
`CLAUDE.md` states each in one line and sends you here. **Read it before
adding a `backdrop-filter`, making a bar `sticky`, animating opacity over
generated media, or putting a list beside an input.**

The through-line: this app puts dozens of animating, blurred, generating media
tiles on one screen. A cost that is invisible on one card is the frame budget
on twelve.

- **A history panel next to an input panel is `memo`'d, and its props are stable.** The history banks are uncapped (only `brollHistory` has a cap), so a working member's gallery is hundreds of rows, each with its own asset lookup — and it sits in the same component as the box they're typing into. One fresh callback identity per render means every character typed re-renders the whole list: measured at ~70 ms (Playground) and ~85 ms (Characters) per keystroke at 300 items, versus ~4–10 ms once the subtree is skipped. `PlaygroundHistoryGrid` and Characters' `GalleryPanel` are the two wired this way — `memo` on the panel, `useCallback` with only stable deps on every handler it takes, and the live draft read through a ref rather than closed over. The React Compiler does this on its own for components it can compile, so the explicit `memo` is a belt-and-braces for the app roots that still bail (see Tech Stack).

- **`backdrop-filter` is for small chrome, and `filter: blur()` never animates.** The one rule behind a whole class of "the app feels laggy" reports, fixed in August 2026. A `backdrop-filter` element is a **backdrop root**: a repaint anywhere inside it invalidates its entire backdrop. `App.tsx`'s workspace pane — which contains the whole workspace — carried `backdrop-blur-xl`, so every animating pixel in every app dragged a full-viewport filter recompute behind it. What it bought was nothing: behind it is `AppBackground`, a smooth dithered gradient, and a blurred smooth gradient is the same gradient. It's a plain unpainted pane now (the rounded, bordered floating "window" frame around it came off in August 2026 too — the pane fills the space between the menu bar and the dock flush, so nothing draws a box around the apps), and removing it is why generation animations stopped stuttering, why zoom stopped lagging (a zoom re-rasters everything, backdrops included), and why images stopped looking blocky — a browser under that much compositing pressure drops its raster scale. Keep `backdrop-filter` on small static chrome over real content, and never put one on a full-window container. **`GeneratingBackdrop`'s three `blur-2xl` blobs are the deliberate exception** and are NOT to be "optimised" again: they were rewritten as radial gradients on exactly this reasoning (three animated blur filters per tile, on a surface whose job is to appear a dozen at a time) and it was reverted within the hour, because a gradient's falloff is a linear ramp where a Gaussian blur's is a smooth curve — the plateau edge reads as banded lobes instead of one soft wash, and this is a glow whose whole job is to look good while someone waits. Their bulk cost is handled where it actually bites, by `offscreen-idle` (`content-visibility: auto`, #459) skipping tiles that are off screen. **The on-screen half was paid down in August 2026 by taking the `scale()` out of the `blob-drift-*` keyframes**, exactly as this note had said to do first: scaling re-rasters a blurred layer every frame, translating one is a compositor move of a texture that already exists, and a clip batch puts a backdrop on every card at once. The reach the scale used to give is baked into each blob's own width/height instead; what's gone is the breathe, and the drift that stayed is the half that reads as movement. The blur itself is still untouchable — if this ever needs to come down further, take an animated blob out entirely rather than lowering `blur-2xl` or going back to gradients.

- **Chrome that must not move lives OUTSIDE the scroller, not `sticky` inside it.** B-Roll's two storyboard strips were `sticky top-0` glass bars inside the scroll port, and they visibly came loose from the top edge on the way back up a long storyboard: a backdrop-filter element re-samples its backdrop on the main thread while the scroller is scrolled by the compositor, so the bar lagged its own container by a frame or two and snapped back. Both are static `shrink-0` rows above the scroll port now — a sibling of the scrolling box, exactly like the panel header above them — which makes the pinning structural rather than something the compositor has to keep agreeing to. Ask first whether the bar ever actually scrolls away: neither of these did (both were pinned at `top-0` from the first pixel), so `sticky` was buying nothing but the artifact. The cost is that content no longer passes under them blurred, so they take the **opaque** fill (glass over a panel background that never moves is a backdrop root's cost for no picture) and keep `relative z-20` so a card's own positioned hover chrome can't paint over them. When a bar genuinely does have to scroll away and come back, `sticky` is still the tool — just don't also make it glass.

- **Pinned panel chrome is glass, and anything that FADES on hover is not.** Two halves of one backdrop-filter rule. A bar that stays put over content it doesn't scroll with (B-Roll's panel header) takes `supports-[backdrop-filter]:bg-surface-0/80 + backdrop-blur-xl + backdrop-saturate-150` over an opaque `bg-surface-0` fallback: content passing under it blurred is what makes it read as a fixed pane of the panel rather than a card-shaped hole, and a nested backdrop-filter DOES blur inside a parent `backdrop-blur-xl` (the earlier "it can't re-blur" note was wrong — what made a translucent strip read as scrolling was too little blur and no saturation). The opposite holds for anything animating its own opacity: a backdrop-filter re-samples its backdrop every frame, so a hover-faded overlay over a real generated still stutters — which is why `TileActionStack` has never had one, and why B-Roll's variation-card shortcut row, tag chip and status badges dropped theirs (an empty card never showed it; a flat face is nothing to blur).
