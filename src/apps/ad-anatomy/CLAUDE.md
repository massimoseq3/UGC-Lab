# Ad Analyzer — `ad-anatomy/`

Create section of UGC OS. This file loads when Claude works with files under `src/apps/ad-anatomy/`; the app-wide rules stay in the root [CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md).

## Job

Ad image or video frame → Breakdown (scorecard + hook / angle / structure) + transcript + visual playbook. The Breakdown card carries **no action row** — it used to ship a fourth field, `CreativeBreakdown.stylePrompt`, a product-agnostic writing brief saved to the Script Bank as `kind: 'style'`; **cut July 2026** because stripping every concrete detail (the thing that made it reusable) left generic DTC platitudes whichever ad went in. Bank rows already saved still render (`BankList`'s `kind === 'style'` branch stays); nothing produces new ones. **All three panels share one header band, and the read's is OUTSIDE its scroller.** The section-jump toggle sits in an `h-[57px]` row that is a SIBLING of the scroll port rather than `sticky top-0` inside it (the app-wide rule in the root CLAUDE.md): it was pinned at the top from the first pixel and never scrolled away, so sticky bought nothing but the chance to come loose from the edge. It stays opaque (`bg-surface-0`, no `backdrop-blur`) — the column has no background of its own, so a translucent bar let the cards scroll visibly through it. The media column carries the **filename pill** in a band of its own at the same height, which is why: the pill used to sit at the FOOT of that column, so the hairline the History rail and the toggle bar already draw stopped two thirds of the way across the screen. The jump targets take `scroll-mt-5` rather than a bar's worth of offset, since nothing overlaps the top of the scroller any more. The upload screen and the analysing screen are deliberately spare — headline + drop zone, then title / the ad playing under the scanning sweep with a **sound toggle** on it (autoplay has to start muted, but the member is watching their own ad). A rotating "Did you know" ad-fact box under the sweep was cut; see git history. What the spare screens DO have to say is **how long this takes** — "Please wait — this can take a couple of minutes", once under the Analyze button (before they commit) and once under the sweep. A whole-video read is one long call with no progress to stream, so an unstated wait reads as a hung page, and members were reloading out of it. The analysing line deliberately stops at "it keeps running if you switch tools" and does NOT claim it survives a refresh: it usually does, but a reload before kie hands back a taskId can't be resumed, and a promise broken once is worse than one never made. A **`PreparingPane`** covers the gap between the Analyze click and the history row landing — an IndexedDB write of the whole clip, during which there was no `selected` row and the screen fell back to a bare upload zone, so the click looked ignored. **Nothing that dies loses the member's upload.** `uploadedRef` now survives both a failure and a refresh-interrupt (it used to be dropped and the asset deleted), so `ErrorPane` leads with **Retry analysis** — `retryAnalysis` reloads the blob, rebuilds the File and re-enqueues — and falls back to "Upload another" only when the source is genuinely gone. The mount-time TTL sweep covers error rows on the same 14-day rule as complete ones, so retained sources stay bounded, and deleting the row purges the asset outright. **The ad sits on the RIGHT and the read on the left** (August 2026): the History rail is already down the left edge, so a media column beside it put two narrow columns between the member and the thing they came to read, and the breakdown — the widest, longest content on the screen — started a third of the way in. The media column stays FIRST in the DOM and moves with `md:order-2`, because the two layouts want different reading orders: on a phone the ad is the anchor you land on and the breakdown scrolls under it, on a desktop the breakdown is what's being read and the ad belongs beside it. **The source player is also the frame grabber.** While a row still holds its `uploadedRef`, the left column plays the real ad, and **Download this frame** under it — at the `px-4 py-2.5` / 16px-icon weight of the `ScriptActionRow` buttons, since it is the media column's only action and a chip-sized one read as an afterthought — hands over the frame the playhead is parked on — full source resolution, PNG, named `<file>-frame-00m01s40.png` — because the alternative members were reaching for is a cropped screenshot of the app window. `captureFrameFromElement` (`utils/videoFrames.ts`) reads the LIVE element rather than loading and seeking a hidden one the way `extractVideoFrame` does: no second decode, and what lands on disk is exactly the frame on screen, which is the whole promise when the member picked the moment themselves. It pauses the player first, or the grab races the click for whichever frame happened to be up. The native controls stay the only scrubber — a second timeline under a third-width player would be a worse one than the browser's. It renders only for `mediaKind === 'video'`, a flag `ResultsView` now takes for the other half of the same problem: an image ad was handed to a `<video>` element regardless and drew an empty player. **The analysis is ONE chat call** (`analyzeAd.ts`): the video inline as the only media part, returning scorecard + breakdown + transcript + scene prompts in one JSON object. It is the **only surface on `CHAT_MODEL_STRONG`** (Gemini 3.6 Flash) and the only one at **MEDIUM** reasoning effort (August 2026 — it ran on the app-wide default at low effort before): everything it returns is read by a person and shot against, so a misread style family or a hedged scene prompt costs a re-shoot rather than a retry, which is the case that tier is kept for. Both knobs live at the top of `analyzeAd.ts` and BOTH transports read them — `kieChatCompletions` defaults to low effort, so the streaming fallback has to be handed `REASONING_EFFORT` explicitly or it quietly analyses at a different effort from the task path. `analysisCost.ts` must track `CHAT_MODEL_ID` too, or the Analyze button quotes one model's rate for another model's call; its `REASONING_TOKENS` allowance is there because thinking is billed as output whether or not `include_thoughts` shows it. It polls on `CHAT_POLL_ATTEMPTS`, not the 5-minute default a whole-video read outlives, and `taskId` on the history row is what a refresh resumes. A **two-pass split is retired** (shipped #261, reverted July 2026): perception (video + ~20 client-side cut keyframes → transcript + per-cut shot log + dossiers) then text-only synthesis. It was written for more shot-level detail and every analysis came back rejected as invalid; budgeting the inline payload and buying a video-only retry (#373) didn't fix it. Its `PerceptionResult` / `SynthesisResult` types and `utils/extractKeyframes.ts` are deleted (see git history); `AdAnatomyHistoryItem.perception` stays as a legacy field nothing writes. If it's ever revisited, reintroduce ONE variable at a time — extra media parts, reasoning effort, and the second call are three separate suspects. **The Scenes card leads with two master blocks** (`MasterVisualStyle` / `MasterVoiceProfile` on `ReverseEngineeredPrompt`; both optional — legacy rows predate them, and a silent ad has no voice). Every scene prompt stays self-contained because each one is fired as its own clip; the masters exist for the two things that must NOT drift between clips. **Visual Style** classifies the ad against B-Roll's own picker list (`styleFamilyMenu()` off `CONTINUOUS_STYLES`, so a style added there is immediately one the analyser can name) plus `'other'`, badges `liveAction` — the "UGC realism or AI animation?" question answered on its own — and carries a brief written to `STYLE_BRIEF_SPEC`, the same five axes `analyzeStyleReferences` distils, so **Save style** drops it straight into the `styles` bank and the member can render their OWN ad in the winner's look (the button tracks the saved BRIEF, not a boolean — the component survives a History switch, and a flag left the next ad reading "Saved"). An unrecognised id normalises to `'other'` rather than through `getContinuousStyle`, whose fallback would label an unknown look "3D Animated". **Voice** is `VOICE_PROFILE_SPEC` (shared with Scripts via `utils/voiceProfile.ts`) plus `delivery` — on-camera sync vs. voiceover over b-roll vs. an interviewer, which is what recreations get wrong most. Both ride the top of Copy All and of the Scripts handoff in the `=== MASTER … ===` shape Scripts already emits; `detectSceneBlueprint` matches scene headers anywhere in a source, so the preamble is safe. **The scene prompts are written for reproduction, not description**: every shot accounts for camera position as geometry, movement, setting, action, lighting, the dialogue heard over it and the on-screen text quoted verbatim; hedges and generics ("a woman", "appears to be") are banned outright, a hard-to-see visual detail is committed to rather than hedged — but a brand name, caption, price or spoken word is never invented — and each scene repeats the full character and product description, since "the same woman as before" is a broken prompt when the scene is fired alone. **A scene prompt is READ as its beats** (`utils/scenePrompt.ts`, rendered by `SceneCard`): the card splits it on the `[0:00–0:03]` markers the prompt contract already asks for, puts each marker in red on its own line — followed by how long that beat RUNS (`rangeDurationLabel` in `utils/timecode.ts`, shared with Scripts' scene headers, which show the same ranges off the same blueprints), since the range is where the beat sits and the duration is what it gets reproduced at — and lifts the quoted verbatim lines out of the prose into their own boxes — the shape Scripts gives a scene, since both are a shot's direction wrapped around the words that get said. Two quote kinds, because one is heard and the other is read: a spoken line takes the scene accent, transcribed on-screen text stays neutral chrome, and each copies on its own. It rewrites nothing — **Copy still hands over `scene.prompt` verbatim**, which is the artifact; this is a reading of it, and one long paragraph of camera geometry with the dialogue buried mid-clause was the thing nobody could scan. It deliberately does NOT reuse Scripts' `splitSpokenLines`: that parser holds the tighter contract (`[CHARACTER] says: "…"`, always double quotes, always a colon), while the analyser transcribes what it heard — single quotes as often as double, `says,` as often as `says:`, a cue that runs on before it hands over ("The voiceover continues over the shot: …"), and quoted on-screen text that is not dialogue at all; bending one parser to serve both would loosen the one holding the tighter contract. Three guards earn their keep: an **unattributed** quote stays in the prose (a scare quote is direction, and promoting it cuts the sentence in half), a **single** quote only closes on a mark not followed by a letter (the prose is full of "the woman's knee"), and a lead that doesn't resolve to something short and name-like renders the box **unlabelled** rather than printing half a clause as a speaker. A prompt with no markers — a single-shot scene, a still — comes back as one untimed beat, which renders as the plain paragraph it always did. The Transcript and Scenes cards each end in a two-button row (`ScriptActionRow`): **Save to Script Bank** + a remix button — **"Remix transcript"** / **"Remix for your product"** — both handing their text to Scripts' Remix box. The labels name what is handed over, not the destination app; the section heading above already says which one you're reading. Their label colour is `text-scripts-text`, never `text-scripts-400` — Scripts' accent is a dark navy (`#24365A`), so a 400 label on its own tint measures ~1.6:1 and reads as a disabled button (still true in light mode, where 400 mixes from the brand navy toward black; dark mode's 400 is now a lift of that block's own 500, but it exists as the hover step for a filled button, not as a text tint). That token is the app-wide rule for **any** text or glyph sitting on a scripts tint. **A third button, "Clone this with my product", is removed** (July 2026): it read as "recreate this ad" but actually dropped the member on the B-Roll page. Its whole pipeline is still wired and unreferenced — `services/adBlueprint.ts` → `AdBlueprintPayload`, targetField `adBlueprint`, consumed by B-Roll as its only scene-staging input — so restoring it is re-adding the button under a label that says where it goes. What it sent is worth keeping in mind if it comes back: **staging, not prompts** — the ad's transcript becomes B-Roll's script and its beat map + shot craft ride `BrollInput.sceneStaging`, the same seam a Script Style format uses, so B-Roll writes fresh prompts against the member's OWN product and character refs. The analysed scene prompts deliberately stay behind — they name the original creator's face and the original brand's packaging (the recreation doctrine requires it), so shipping them alongside the member's references would put two people and two products in frame and render a competitor's label as the member's product.
