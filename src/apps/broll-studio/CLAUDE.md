# B-Roll — `broll-studio/`

Create section of UGC OS. This file loads when Claude works with files under `src/apps/broll-studio/`; the app-wide rules stay in the root [CLAUDE.md](../../../CLAUDE.md).

## Job

**Continuous is opt-in and ships OFF** (`stores/appVisibilityStore.ts`'s `OPTIONAL_FEATURES`, switched in Settings → **Experimental**; per-browser, never cloud-synced). It gates on ONE derived value — `BrollStudio` reads the flag and hands everything downstream `mode = enabled ? storedMode : 'line'`, so the toggle, the right panel, Generate and the import modal are all covered by that line and nothing else in the app has to ask. Two surfaces do read the flag itself: `InputPanel` drops the mode toggle **and its whole 57px header bar** (Massimo's call — that bar exists only to hold the toggle, so with one mode left an empty band with a rule across it was 57px spent lining up with a header that has something in it), and `RightPanel` filters Continuous rows out of History and drops the "Line by Line" qualifier from the Storyboard tab. Nothing is written, migrated or deleted: the stored mode, the keyframe chain, its selections and its history rows are all left as they are, so switching it back on reopens the session exactly where it was — the same promise Outliers makes one level up, and the same reason History hides a Continuous row rather than orphaning it (there is no mode left to open one in, exactly like `isRetiredOneShotRow`).

**The scene header CENTRES on a phone** (August 2026). Its desktop shape is a masthead — take number, hairline rule, then the line running away to the right, batch buttons on the far edge — which needs a column wide enough for the rule to divide something. A phone doesn't have one, so the number sits over the line, the rule goes, the quote is centred, and the two per-scene batch buttons centre under it. What survives is the take number and the words, which is what the header is for. **The line itself is upright Instrument Serif, not italic** (September 2026, Massimo's call) — only the numeral is italic, which is also what `ContinuousView` had always done, so the two deliveries now set the same sentence the same way. It was swapped to the app's own sans for a day in the same month and put straight back (Massimo's call, both directions): the serif line is the masthead, and the four places it is drawn — `ScenesView`, `ContinuousView` and both detail modals — have to move together or one delivery sets the sentence differently from the other. **The `LINE N` chip is gone at every width** (August 2026, Massimo's call): it printed the scene's number two inches from the 48px italic numeral that is the masthead's own left half. A phone had already dropped it for that reason, and a repeat is a repeat at any width. The line editor under it carries **no explainer** either: three lines saying that saving is free, instant and doesn't touch the shots is what saving a line has always done, and reading it every time you fix a typo is clutter rather than reassurance.

**Every card workspace can fire up to four TAKES of its own prompt** (`BatchCountStepper` in the per-line card modal's Image / Video / Animate footers, Continuous' frame modal and Continuous' clip modal; one shared persisted count, `ai-ugc-lab:broll:card-takes`). The axis matters: a scene's three **variations** and a frame's three **concepts** are three different IDEAS for the beat, written by the storyboard call — three **takes** are three rolls of the same one, which is what a card's own gallery exists to choose between and the one thing these modals could not ask for. It needs no new machinery because `CardState` already carries parallel `inFlightImages` / `inFlightVideos` queues keyed per generation, so a batch is N pushes onto them and each take resumes across a refresh on its own. **Not on the header batches** (`Generate all images` / `Animate all stills` / `Generate all videos`): those already multiply by scene × option, so a count there would turn a twelve-clip run into thirty-six. Video takes cost far more than image takes, which is why the Generate button beside the stepper prices the whole run.

**Clip length follows the SPOKEN line, not a setting — and only under Dialogue Clips** (`services/clipDuration.ts`, August 2026). A card whose character speaks its line to camera is as long as the words take to say. It was a flat 5s on every card, which is wrong in both directions and was reported as both: a twenty-word line crammed into 5s comes back with the character gabbling, and a five-word hook stretched over the same 5s comes back slow and dead. **The estimate shipped applying to every card in both deliveries and was pulled back to the dialogue cards within the day.** A B-Roll Clips card has no words in it — it's cutaway footage a voiceover is laid over in the edit — so nothing about its length follows from the line it was written for, and a word count there is a number invented for a clip that isn't saying them; those cards keep the flat 5s (snapped down) they always had, their duration chip has no Auto row, and a silent run's batch dialog pins one length exactly as it did before. `speaksItsLine` is the one test and it's `tag === 'DIALOGUE'` — the same predicate the voice profile and the anchor chain already go by, so a hand-added option (tagged ACTION, silent everywhere else in the app) is silent here too. `spokenClipSeconds` is `words / 2.4 + 0.5s` of lead-in/tail, floored at 4s and capped at 15s (past that, splitting the line in the scene editor beats paying for a 20s clip); `autoClipSeconds` snaps that onto the model's own ladder, to the **NEAREST** rung. **It ran 1–2s long on every card at first, and the fix was to stop rounding up three times over**: a full second of lead/tail, a `Math.ceil` on top of it, then a snap UP. Only one round-up survives — the snap, which is unavoidable — and it's a nearest now, because a ladder is coarse (…5, 6, 8, 10, 12…) and an estimate overshooting a rung by a third of a second was buying a whole extra rung. There's no truncation to protect against anyway: the video model FITS the speech to the clip it's given, so a rung short reads as a brisker delivery (the UGC register) while a rung long is dead air at the end of every cut. Measured on Grok's ladder that's 1–2s off a typical line and never longer than before. (The app-wide `snapVideoDuration` still rounds DOWN, which is right for a default nobody chose; `snapVideoDurationUp` survives for a length the member PINNED, where their number must not shrink under them.) Deliberately a word count rather than a number the storyboard model answers with: it's free, deterministic and debuggable, it costs no prompt surface and no parser, and it's the same ~2.4 words/sec pace Scripts writes its word budgets against, so a script written for a beat and the clip rendered for it agree by construction. **Continuous has estimated its scene lengths this way since it shipped** — `sceneDuration` is now that shared estimator with its own 3s floor (quick punchy beats), because the two modes cut the same script into the same beats and had no business disagreeing about how long a line takes to say. A dialogue card is **Auto** until the member picks a number in its duration chip (`CardState.cardVideoDurationAuto`): `cardClipSeconds(card, line, model, { spoken })` is the single place that decision lives, so the chip's trigger ("Auto · 8s"), the modal's credit estimates, the batch dialog's per-card pricing and the generation itself can't drift — and `spoken` is a required argument rather than a defaulted one, since a call site that forgot it would quietly put Auto back on the silent cards. Auto survives a model swap — the estimate re-derives onto the new ladder rather than keeping the old model's number — while a pinned length keeps the old snap-down clamp, since that one is the member's. On a silent card the same flag reads as "did anybody pick this number": true means the value in the slot was derived rather than chosen, so `silentClipSeconds` ignores it and returns the 5s default — which is what un-does the day the estimate applied to everything, without touching a length the member really did set. Cards persisted before the flag land on unpicked only when they hold one of the two values the old seeder could produce (5, or the 4 it snapped to on a ladder that skips 5): a stored value is a real pick everywhere else in the app, but here every card stored one whether or not anyone chose it, so those two ARE the unpicked case.

**A benefit is a picture, a spec is not — and the product context is labelled so it reads that way** (`buildProductContext` in `services/productAngles.ts`, August 2026). Massimo asked for the Scripts benefits-over-features fix to be carried across, and the copywriting half of it has nothing to attach to here: **this app never writes the copy.** The script arrives already written and rule 1 copies every `<LINE>` verbatim ("use the script's exact words in the script's order… never paraphrase, add, or reorder"), Continuous the same. What DID carry across is the cause. The product context was one flat sentence — `Product: … USPs: … Benefits: … Key specs: …` — pasted into Line-by-Line's user prompt with **no framing whatsoever** (Continuous at least said "this is the ADVERTISED product"), which is the same bug Scripts had: unlabelled product material carries no job, and the spec list is the most concrete thing in the prompt. The failure it produces here is VISUAL — hand a model a spec and ask it for a picture and it draws the spec: the claim printed on the wrapper, the nutrition panel held to the lens, the number across the frame. So each field now states what it is FOR in a pipeline whose output is a shot, and the string says out loud that **the attached photo describes the product and this text does not** (rule 8's "the product reference image is the source of truth" had no line connecting it to the spec list sitting in the same prompt). It is built in `productAngles.ts` and used by all five consumers through the one string — both storyboard prompts, `frameBriefBlock`, and the two per-card rewrites — so the labels are the single lever. Empty bank fields are now **dropped** rather than rendered as a labelled blank: only `keySpecs` was ever guarded, so a product saved without USPs shipped the model a literal `USPs: .` The prompt side is the visual translation of the same rule — **A BENEFIT IS A PICTURE, A SPEC IS NOT**, under SHOW-DON'T-TELL in both system instructions: when a line makes a promise, show the promise KEPT inside a life, and when the shot IS the product it is the product being USED or the result it left behind, never presented and never its packaging read like a spec sheet. Two lens definitions named the object ahead of the outcome and were reordered — `PRODUCT` ("the product itself or its visible result") and `PROOF`, whose "visible evidence the claim is real" resolves to the ingredient list when a spec list is in the same prompt — plus self-check 6b, which is what makes the rule survive contact with a full Key Specs field. **None of this is measured**; it is prompt work, so judge it on a real storyboard before tuning further. **September 2026:** the Bank removed its Key specs & facts field outright — the one this string called its weakest material — so `buildProductContext` no longer sends a spec list at all. In its place it sends the brief's **Before / after**, which is two first-person paragraphs of a day before and a day after: already written as moments (a room, a time of day, a thing in someone's hands), which is a shot list in everything but name and the only material here that describes anything a camera could point at. Its label says to stage the concrete DETAIL and never the feeling it is named for. Nothing else from the brief comes across — objections and the rest are words for the script to say, and this file's whole rule is that nothing here becomes words.

**The storyboard call is a JOB, not an awaited fetch** (`services/storyboardRun.ts`, August 2026). Writing the prompts was the one generation in the app that showed nothing anywhere until it landed: every other surface pushes an in-flight tile before the network call — that's what makes History the queue view — while this one left History empty and the panel spinning, and a reload threw away a call kie had already billed for. Both halves are fixed by the same move, and it's the Ad Analyzer's shape (`analysisQueue.ts`) rather than a new one. **The history row is written FIRST**, in `storyboardStatus: 'writing'`, so the session appears in History from the press, reports "Writing storyboard…" under a `GeneratingPulseRing`, counts into the "N sessions rendering" chip, and keeps doing all three while the member works in another app. **The call itself goes through kie's task transport where the picked writer model has a job route** (`createTask` → `recordInfo`, the model named by `chatSlug ?? id`): the taskId is persisted on the row, so opening B-Roll after a reload re-attaches the poll (`resumeStoryboard`) and the storyboard lands in the workspace as if nothing happened. A model with no job route 400s at `createTask` and falls back to streaming — that path is honest but unresumable, and the console says which one ran. **A row nothing can finish is STRANDED, never left pulsing** (`isStoryboardStranded`): on mount, a 'writing' row with no taskId is flipped to `'error'` when it's this browser's own pending run, and only after `STORYBOARD_TTL_MS` (30 min) otherwise — a row synced from another device may still be writing over there. Three rules hold it together: **the workspace is not touched until a storyboard exists** (the old commit discipline — rotating the session up front left a failed call showing the previous scenes stripped of every image), so `adoptStoryboard` is the one place a result lands, in both the live and the resumed case; **the response is parsed against the ROW, never the live panel** (`parseStoryboard` reads `lineDelivery` / `styleId` / `styleBrief` / `continuousModelId` off it), so a storyboard that finishes after a reload — or after the member changed the toggle while waiting — parses exactly as it would have at the moment it fired; and **a failed row keeps its friendly message on the card** and is not clickable in either state, since it holds nothing to restore (`handleSelectHistory` guards on `storyboardStatus`). A missing kie key is checked before the row is written — a failure we can see coming has no business leaving a dead session in History. `buildBrollMessages` / `buildBrollResult` and `buildContinuousMessages` are the split-out halves of what used to be `generateBroll` / `generateContinuous`; the transport lives in one place now, and `extractChatTaskText` (in `utils/kie.ts`) is shared with the Ad Analyzer so a result shape learned by one is read by both.

**A storyboard that stops short of the script asks for the rest** (`services/storyboardCompletion.ts`, September 2026). This is the longest single output in the app by a wide margin — three paragraph-length prompts AND three motion lines for every line of the script, under a prompt that explicitly forbids trimming a detail to keep the answer short — so it is the one call that routinely runs into a model's output ceiling. When it did, nothing noticed: the tag readers in `xmlBlocks` are deliberately tolerant of a missing closing tag, so half a storyboard rendered as a SHORT storyboard, with no error anywhere, and the member fired paid image and video generations against scenes their script never got. Reported as B-Roll "cutting out half way through". **Two tests, because there are two ways to come back short and neither catches the other.** The transport now says when the model hit its ceiling — the streaming one always threw `TruncatedResponseError`, and the jobs one (the one this call actually takes) reads the same stop reason as of `chatTaskHitTokenLimit`. But a model that simply STOPS EARLY reports a clean finish, and that is not hypothetical: Gemini 3.7 Flash was promoted and reverted inside a day over exactly this symptom. So the second test measures the answer against the SCRIPT, using a rule the prompt already enforces — every `<LINE>` is the script's own words, in order — and calls a storyboard short when its lines account for under **85%** of the script's words. The floor is not 1 because the model may drop a connecting word at a split; a run that stopped half way sits near 0.5, nowhere near it, and the script is normalised first (`spokenScript` strips `[stage directions]`, `(beats)` and `HOOK:` labels) so scaffolding the model was never asked to quote can't read as a shortfall. **The answer to both is to ask for the rest, not to error**: the partial goes back as the assistant's own turn, trimmed to its last COMPLETE scene, with one instruction to carry on — which keeps everything a fresh run would lose, most importantly the dialogue anchor take every VAR_1 has to stay inside. Up to `MAX_STORYBOARD_CONTINUATIONS` (3) rounds, stopping the moment a round adds no new scene, and the text kept is always the longest we ever held. A continuation's taskId is deliberately **not** persisted over the first one — a reload mid-continuation would otherwise re-attach to the tail and adopt half a storyboard's second half as the whole thing. **A resumed run is checked but never continued** (its messages went down with the previous page, and rebuilding them from the row would drop the product and character context it was written against), and anything still short after the loop is **adopted with a warning that REPLACES the ready toast** (`STORYBOARD_INCOMPLETE_WARNING`) rather than failed: what came back is real work the member paid for and its scenes are usable, but two toasts where the first says "ready" is how someone reads past the one that matters.

**A failed generation that still holds a kie taskId is an INTERRUPTION, not a failure** (August 2026, `cardDetailParts`' `FailedTile` / `InFlightFailureRow` / `keepInFlight` in `cardState.ts`). kie ran the generation and billed for it; what died was our side of the hand-off — the CDN download stalled, the browser couldn't decode the blob, the Wi-Fi dropped between the render finishing and the file landing. Retry has resumed rather than re-fired since `resumeInFlightVideo` shipped, so it was already free; what was missing is that **nothing said so**, and three things conspired to lose the clip anyway. First, the copy: the browser-side failures (`probeVideoBlob`'s metadata timeout, a rejected blob) carried the word "timed out" and fell through `humanizeError`'s generic timeout rule to *"it's likely still running on kie.ai — check there before generating it again"*, which describes a different failure and reads as "Retry will charge you again". They have their own rule now, the probe budget went 8s → 30s (it is a LOCAL decode, and it was blowing when a whole batch landed at once), and the raw error is `console.error`'d before it's humanized — the friendly copy is all the entry keeps, so without that every one of these looks identical in a bug report. Second, the tile: a resumable entry renders **amber "Not saved"** with "Retry is free — kie.ai already made this one" instead of the red FAILED alarm, and its **Dismiss takes two clicks** (arms into "Discard?", reverts after 3s — the `TileDeleteButton` idiom), because dismissing is the one move that really does throw a paid clip away. A dead entry — one that never reached kie — keeps the red treatment and a one-click Dismiss, since nothing was charged. Third, and the actual data loss: `backfillCardState` swept every in-flight entry at 30 min (images) / 60 min (videos), **including the ones holding a taskId**, so a member who came back the next morning had them deleted on hydrate before any resume could run. kie keeps a result for **3 days**, so `keepInFlight` now gives a resumable entry that long and leaves the short sweep for entries with nothing to go back for. The resume passes in `ScenesView` / `ContinuousView` already walked errored entries (they keep their taskId) and now re-run on `useReconnectTick` as well as on mount — a clip lost to a Wi-Fi blip lands on its own when the connection returns, with `claimTask` stopping the extra pass from double-polling. `online` only, deliberately: a failed attempt re-toasts, and a genuinely dead task would re-report itself on every tab switch.

**A Line-by-Line prompt is ONE FRAME, not a sequence — and the guarantee is appended at fire time, not just asked for** (August 2026, `PROMPT_FORMAT_CORE` in `services/generateBroll.ts` + `services/realism.ts`). One paragraph feeds both the still and the clip it animates into, so a prompt that narrates a CHAIN of beats — the drawer slides open, then the hand sweeps the pile out, then the drawer knocks shut — hands an image model three moments and it draws all three: a strip of stacked panels, a split screen, a before-and-after, one impossible frame with three pairs of hands in it. Reported as "weird results", and it was never the image model's fault — the **reference example in the system prompt was itself written as a sequence**, and a model copies the example over the rules. So all three example prompts are single frozen instants now (mid-bite, mid-sweep, mid-swallow), `PROMPT_FORMAT_CORE` leads with **ONE FRAME, ONE ACTION** (length is spent on DETAIL, never on more events; if a prompt could describe two shots, add specificity, never another moment), rule 7 went from "CONSTANT MOTION" to **ONE MOTION, CAUGHT MID-WAY** — the old wording asked for movement and never named a ceiling, which is what let one prompt carry three — the self-check gained "count the moments", and Enhance was told that detail means the prop, the grip and the light, never another beat (it was the one path explicitly asked to come back *richer*, so it grew prompts by adding events). Continuous never had this bug: its `KEYFRAME_FORMAT` has said "THIS IS A START FRAME … its OPENING INSTANT" since it shipped, and Line-by-Line was the gap. **The mechanical half is `withSingleFrame` / `withSingleTake`**, appended in `startImageTask` / `startVideoTask` exactly like `withNoOnScreenText` and for the same reason: a writing rule binds the chat model and nothing else, so a hand-edited prompt, an imported storyboard, a card written before the rule, or a session resumed from history was uncovered. Both are unconditional rather than gated on `noRealism` — a keyframe is one frame in any look. Video takes the TAKE clause, not the frame one: a clip has time and may play its one action out, what it may not do is cut, which is the same thing B-Roll has always said by never sending `multiShots`.

**A still and the clip it animates into are TWO prompts, and the second one is one or two sentences** (August 2026, `MOTION_FORMAT` + `PromptVariation.motionPrompt` in `services/generateBroll.ts`, `CardState.animateMotion`). The Animate tab fired the card's own image prompt, which is by construction a frozen instant — one action caught before it lands, per the ONE FRAME note above. Handed back to a video model that is already *holding* that frame as its start image, the whole paragraph reads as "draw this picture", so the model spends the clip re-rendering what it can already see and the character barely moves: reported as "when I animate it the character doesn't do much". The storyboard now writes a **second, much shorter prompt per variation** — `<MOTION>`, one or two sentences of the same single action playing OUT, ending on a clause saying the camera stays locked — and the Animate tab is that field, not the shot paragraph. Everything else is unchanged: the Image tab and the Video tab still fire the still prompt, because a from-scratch clip has no frame and genuinely needs the picture described. **THE CAMERA NEVER MOVES, and the motion never says so** (both Massimo's calls, in that order): this is UGC filmed on a propped phone, a video model given no camera direction invents a push-in on every shot, and a camera move is also the fastest way to lose the character's face — the model has to invent geometry the start frame never showed it. So `MOTION_FORMAT` bans every push, pull, zoom, pan, tilt, orbit, track, drift, sway and rack focus **by name** — and bans "the camera holds steady" with them, because the box is for what MOVES and a sentence about a camera deliberately doing nothing is a sentence the member reads past every time. The lock is guaranteed downstream instead, by **`withLockedCamera`** in `services/realism.ts`, appended at fire time beside `applyStyleToPrompt` and the voice profile so the persisted prompt stays what the member typed. It sits alongside the three clauses already there and for the same reason — a writing rule binds the chat model and nothing else, so a hand-typed motion, an import or a resumed session was uncovered — and it is armed by the presence of the motion argument, i.e. by this being an animate run: the **Video tab is deliberately excluded**, since a from-scratch clip has no frame to hold still. Three things follow. Continuous has worked this way since it shipped (`ContinuousConcept.motionPrompt` → the frame card's `animateMotion`), so this is that idea brought across, not a new one — the camera rule is the one place the two deliberately differ. **An empty motion falls back to the still prompt** at fire time (`animatePrompt` in `VariationCard`, mirrored on the modal's disabled gate), which is what a session generated before this shipped, an import written against the old envelope, and a hand-added blank option all are — taking Animate away from those would be worse than the behaviour they already had. The modal used to spell that fallback out in a line under the box; it came off (Massimo's call) — it explained a case the member never has to think about, under a field that needs no instructions. And a **dialogue** motion carries the spoken line verbatim in quotes exactly as the shot prompt does, because it is the whole prompt an animated talking clip fires with; that makes it the fourth place `handleEditSceneLine` has to swap the words, alongside the scene, the variation's prompt and the card's `editablePrompt`.
